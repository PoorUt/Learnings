Why We Read Left → Right

It's not a choice — it's what the derivative means

The short version:

Imagine you're standing on a hill and walking left → right along the x-axis (the direction x increases).

🔴 Local Max = You were going uphill, then you start going downhill. You just passed a peak.
    f′ goes from + → −

🟢 Local Min = You were going downhill, then you start going uphill. You just passed a valley.
    f′ goes from − → +

Why left-to-right? Because the derivative is defined as the rate of change as x increases. "f′ > 0" literally means "y goes up when x goes right." It's not a convention we chose — it's what the math says.

Even if you imagine walking right-to-left, the peak is still the highest nearby point and the valley is still the lowest. The function's shape doesn't change — only your perspective does.
